Does rice raise blood sugar? Portions, swaps and what helps

By Vishnu Purushothaman, who makes Companion · Checked against the sources below on October 19, 2026

Yes. Rice is mostly starch, which your body turns into glucose. A cup of cooked white rice carries about 45 g of carbohydrate, so a third of a cup is about 15 g. Portion matters most; eating it with protein and vegetables, starting with the vegetables, and a short walk afterwards all soften the rise.

White or brown?

Brown rice keeps its bran, so it has more fibre and tends to arrive a little more slowly. It's not a free pass: a cup of brown rice carries as much carbohydrate as white, or a little more. The portion still decides most of the rise.

Three things that soften the rise

  • Order. Eat the vegetables and protein first and the rice last. In several small trials this gave a noticeably smaller rise with exactly the same food on the plate.
  • Company. Rice on its own rises fastest. With chicken, fish, tofu, beans or a big portion of vegetables it slows down.
  • A walk. Ten to fifteen minutes on your feet soon after the meal; it doesn't need to be brisk.

Cooled rice

When cooked rice is cooled in the fridge, part of its starch becomes resistant starch, which digests more like fibre, and it stays that way when reheated. Studies show a modest, variable reduction in the rise, not a transformation, so portion still matters most. Keep cooked rice cold and eat it within a day: rice left warm can cause food poisoning.

Your rice, your readings

People respond differently to the same plate. The way to know is to check: log a rice meal, check one to two hours after you start eating, and compare a few meals over a couple of weeks.
