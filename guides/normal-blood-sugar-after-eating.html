What is a normal blood sugar after eating?

By Vishnu Purushothaman, who makes Companion · Checked against the sources below on October 19, 2026

For many adults with diabetes, a common target is under 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L) one to two hours after the start of a meal, and 80–130 mg/dL (4.4–7.2 mmol/L) before meals. Targets are set individually, so yours should come from your care team.

These are the figures most guidelines start from. Your care team may set tighter or looser ones depending on your age, your medication and how likely lows are for you.

When to check

Glucose normally rises after eating and is highest about one to two hours after the start of the meal, so time the check from your first bite, not from when you finished. A reading at two hours is the one most targets refer to.

What changes the rise

  • How much carbohydrate, and what kind. Portion matters most: a third of a cup of cooked rice or pasta, a slice of bread or half a cup of mashed potato each carry about 15 g.
  • What it is eaten with. Fibre, protein and fat slow the rise. Eating the vegetables and protein first and the starch last gave a smaller rise in several small trials, with the same food on the plate.
  • What you do afterwards. A 10 to 15 minute walk soon after a meal is one of the most reliable ways to soften it.
  • Everything else. Illness, stress and poor sleep tend to push readings up; so can missed or late medication.

One high reading is a fact, not a verdict

A single reading over 180 after a big meal is common and tells you about that meal. What is worth bringing to your care team is the pattern over a few weeks: which meals, which times of day, how often.
