Does walking after eating lower blood sugar?

By Vishnu Purushothaman, who makes Companion · Checked against the sources below on October 19, 2026

Yes. A 10 to 15 minute walk soon after a meal is one of the most reliable ways to soften the rise that follows it, because working muscle takes up glucose without needing much insulin. It doesn't need to be brisk, and it pays most after the largest meal of the day.

Why it works

Muscles that are working pull glucose out of the blood on their own, through a route that needs little insulin. A walk while the meal is being absorbed puts that to use at exactly the moment glucose is arriving.

How much, and when

  • Soon after eating: within about half an hour of finishing, while glucose is still rising.
  • Ten to fifteen minutes is enough to see a difference. Short walks after each meal did better than one longer walk at another time of day in a trial of people with type 2 diabetes.
  • Gentle is fine. A stroll, the dishes and a walk round the block, pacing on a phone call: light movement still counts.
  • After the biggest meal is where it pays most, which for many people is dinner.

When to take care

  • If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea, activity can bring on a low, sometimes hours later. Carry fast-acting carbohydrate and follow your care team's advice.
  • With type 1 diabetes, if you're above about 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L), check ketones first, and don't exercise if they are raised.

See it in your own numbers

Compare readings after dinners with a walk against dinners without, over a couple of weeks. The difference, or the lack of one, is yours.
